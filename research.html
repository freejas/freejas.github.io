Research

Broadly, my research is concerned with how our social environment shapes — and is in turn shaped by — our individual psychology. I explore these questions with quantitative and qualitative methods in cross-cultural psychology, comparing samples across the United States, Japan, and beyond, and with large-scale data, including publicly available datasets and novel datasets built through web scraping.

Research Areas

Relational Mobility

The core thread of my work: how the perceived ease of forming new relationships — and leaving unsatisfying ones — shapes social perception, judgment, and behavior across cultures. My PhD dissertation examined relational mobility as a socio-ecological foundation of prosocial minds and behaviors, and my current multi-national project tests when relational mobility predicts prosociality across the USA, Japan, China, and Poland.

Empathy & Prosociality

How do cross-cultural differences in empathy — empathic concern and positive empathy — help explain differences in prosociality between nations? I study this through work on kindness, responses to social exclusion (including an ongoing fMRI investigation of neural responses to exclusion), and helping intentions across cultures.

Honor

How do cultural and personal values shape perceptions of apology after wrongdoing? As part of the HONORLOGIC project at the University of Sussex, I study honor, face, and dignity cultural logics across 15–16 countries, including how personal and cultural honor values divergently predict the perceived impact of an apology.

Pathogen Prevalence & Mate Preferences

Applying evolutionary psychology and human behavioral ecology, I examine whether regional differences in pathogen prevalence shape racial preferences in dating and marriage. This work, published in PNAS Nexus (2026), shows that US states higher in pathogen prevalence have fewer interracial marriages and more racially-restrictive dating preferences.

Societal Development

With Kuba Krys at the Polish Academy of Sciences, I study visions of a good society — how people across societies and regions prioritize development pathways, and how much people within a country agree or disagree about those priorities.

Medical Decision-Making & Risk Perception

As an Applied Behavioral Scientist at Simply Rational GmbH in Berlin (2021–2023), I worked with international clients in the health-care sector on cross-cultural differences in risk perception among medical professionals, and on behavioral economics approaches — such as nudging — to improve outcomes for patients recovering from surgery or managing chronic disease.

Software

processdiagram

An R package that creates publication-quality path diagrams from PROCESS mediation results (Models 4, 6, 81, and custom b-matrices). It wraps a user-supplied PROCESS function, imports save=2 files, or fits supported models with an independent native engine.

Available on GitHub: github.com/freejas/processdiagram
